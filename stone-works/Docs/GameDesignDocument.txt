STONEWORKS — GAME DESIGN DOCUMENT
=================================

Purpose
-------
This document defines what StoneWorks is and how it should play.
It describes game rules, player experience, content and major design decisions.
Technical implementation, code ownership and system communication belong in TechnicalArchitecture.txt.
Physical file/folder organization belongs in GodotProjectStructure.txt.


1. GAME VISION
--------------

StoneWorks is a portrait Android idle/incremental mining and management game.

Core fantasy:
- Build a profitable mining operation.
- Watch satisfying explosions, falling rocks and gems, machines and workers.
- Collect beautiful colored gemstones and become increasingly wealthy.
- Feel meaningful progress even while doing very little manually.

The game should feel:
- satisfying
- relaxing
- rewarding
- visually alive
- progressively richer and more powerful

The main visual attractions are colorful gem sorting, physical stones/gems, explosions and the growing Sand Tower.

Target platform: Android first.
Orientation: Portrait.
Primary mode: Offline / single-player.
Genre: Idle + incremental + simulation + management.


2. CORE GAMEPLAY LOOP
---------------------

Primary loop:

Mine → stones appear → physics moves resources → machines process stones → gems/sand are produced → logistics workers transport resources → sell gems → buy workers/upgrades → mine more efficiently → repeat.

Player actions:
- Assign workers between Mining and Logistics.
- Trigger dynamite explosions.
- Buy workers and upgrades.
- Sell gems.
- Manage progression and prestige.
- Watch or interact with the simulation.

Automatic activity:
- Mining workers perform mining jobs.
- Logistics workers transport resources.
- Stones and gems fall and collide physically.
- Machines process resources.
- The market changes over time.
- Idle/offline production continues according to the final offline rules.

The game must contain meaningful choices rather than only automatic number growth.


3. WORLD
--------

The main scene is a horizontal industrial mining area presented in portrait view.

From left to right:
1. Mine / mining field
2. Processing machine area
3. Six gem warehouses
4. Sand collection / Sand Tower area

The mine uses a grid for terrain gameplay data. Its visible terrain is a rounded, attractive representation of that grid.

The mine should support continued progression without requiring an infinitely long walk. The exact long-term mine-expansion/reset model is still undecided.

World objects include:
- terrain
- workers
- stones
- gems
- machines
- warehouses
- sand storage/tower

The world should feel physically connected: resources fall, collide and accumulate rather than appearing as purely abstract numbers.


4. RESOURCES
------------

Primary resources:
- Stones
- Gems
- Sand
- Money
- Dynamite

Gems have six colors:
- Red
- Orange
- Yellow
- Green
- Blue
- Purple

Stones are produced by mining. A stone contains potential gem value and must be processed/grinded before its contents are recovered.

Grinding/processing also produces Sand.

Resource rules:
- Logical resource ownership is separate from physical representation.
- Resources may become physical objects when they enter the world.
- Stored resources do not need to remain physical objects.

Exact stone composition, gem probabilities, sand yield and resource tiers remain to be balanced.


5. MINING
---------

Mining is performed by Mining workers using explosives.

Dynamite:
- creates an explosion in the mine
- has a randomized explosion shape
- destroys affected terrain-grid cells
- causes unsupported/invalid terrain to collapse or be removed according to the mine rules
- creates resources from destroyed terrain

Initial rule:
- Every destroyed terrain tile produces one Stone.

Stones spawn at the destroyed location and fall under gravity.

Mining must eventually define:
- terrain types
- mining depth/progression
- resource probabilities
- dynamite costs and availability
- explosion size/shape rules
- how unsupported terrain is determined
- how the mine expands or resets


6. PHYSICS
----------

Physics exists primarily to make the game satisfying, readable and believable.
It does not need to be a perfect real-world simulation.

Physical interactions include:
- stones with the ground
- stones with other stones
- gems with the ground
- gems with other gems
- resources with relevant machine/environment geometry
- workers with terrain and world geometry

Objects may fall, roll, collide and settle.

Performance is more important than perfect physical accuracy. The game should remain responsive on target Android hardware when many resources are present.

The exact maximum simultaneous physical objects and overload behavior are development constraints to be established during implementation/testing.


7. WORKERS
----------

There are two worker roles:

Mining
- perform mining jobs
- use dynamite
- do not carry resources

Logistics
- carry resources
- collect processed resources
- deliver resources to storage
- sort gems into the correct warehouses
- transport Sand to its destination

Starting workers: 2.
Maximum planned workers: 20.

The player can move workers between roles through the worker panel:
- left arrow: Logistics → Mining
- right arrow: Mining → Logistics

Workers must be able to traverse the mine, including climbing up and down terrain, while respecting terrain collision.

Worker progression currently focuses on increasing worker count. Additional worker abilities are optional future design.


8. JOBS AND LOGISTICS
---------------------

Mining and Logistics are different gameplay domains.

Mining creates mining work.
Logistics determines what should be transported and creates transport work.
A generic Job System handles job lifecycle and assignment.

Typical logistics flow:

Resource becomes available
→ logistics identifies required destination
→ transport job is created
→ worker claims job
→ worker collects resource
→ worker carries resource
→ worker delivers resource
→ resource enters storage/processing flow.

The player primarily influences logistics by choosing the number of Logistics workers.


9. MACHINES AND PROCESSING
--------------------------

Machines transform resources into more valuable resources or otherwise improve the production chain.

Initial important machine:
- Grinder / processing machine that processes Stones and produces Gems and Sand.

Machine upgrades primarily improve effectiveness, initially through faster processing.

Potential future machines should only be added when they create meaningful gameplay. Possible examples include:
- sorter
- conveyor
- gem cutter
- separator
- furnace

Each machine should have a clear input, output, processing rule and upgrade path.


10. GEM STORAGE
---------------

There are six dedicated gem warehouses, one for each color.

Order from right to left:
Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Purple.

The warehouses are tall structures. Logistics workers climb to the top and drop gems into the corresponding warehouse.

Storage should be visually readable and satisfying. Exact capacities and whether capacity can be upgraded remain to be decided.


11. SELLING AND MARKET
----------------------

Gems are sold for Money.

The intended market behavior is dynamic:
- each gem has a base value
- selling a gem causes its current price to fall significantly
- prices have smaller random fluctuations
- prices recover toward their base value over time
- saving gems and selling in larger batches should be a viable strategy

A possible system where the highest-value gem rotates periodically was considered, but is NOT currently a confirmed rule.

The final market model must answer:
- how often prices update
- how strongly selling affects price
- how fast recovery occurs
- whether gem base values differ
- whether one gem can become temporarily more valuable than the others


12. ECONOMY
----------

Money is the main spending currency.

Money is earned by selling Gems.

Money is spent on:
- additional workers
- machine upgrades
- other progression systems as they are introduced

The economy must maintain a satisfying progression curve:
- early upgrades should arrive frequently
- later upgrades should require meaningful accumulation
- production should grow without becoming effectively infinite too quickly
- progression should not become stalled for unreasonable periods

Exact prices, production rates and scaling are balancing data, not fixed design yet.


13. PROGRESSION AND UPGRADES
----------------------------

Primary progression:
- more workers
- more effective machines
- improved mining/processing capability
- access to later content

Machine upgrades currently focus on processing speed.

Other possible upgrades may affect:
- mining efficiency
- dynamite efficiency
- processing speed
- storage
- logistics efficiency
- gem yield

Upgrade effects and costs must be defined as the economy is balanced.


14. PRESTIGE
-----------

Prestige is intended as the long-term reset/progression system.

The current concept:
- normal progression is reset or partially reset
- permanent progression is retained
- Prestige unlocks stronger long-term advantages
- Autosell is one planned prestige unlock

The Sand Tower is a persistent visual representation of long-term progress and continues growing across prestige cycles.

The exact prestige currency, reset scope, permanent upgrades and unlock requirements remain to be designed.


15. SAND TOWER
-------------

Sand is transported to a Sand collector and used to build a Tower in the background.

The Tower is persistent and grows as the player produces/contributes Sand.

Its primary purpose is currently:
- visible long-term progression
- a satisfying persistent reward
- a visual representation of accumulated production

Whether the Tower has additional gameplay effects is undecided.


16. IDLE AND OFFLINE PROGRESSION
--------------------------------

StoneWorks is primarily an idle game and must support progress without constant interaction.

Offline progress should be calculated rather than simulating every physical object for the entire offline period.

The final design must define:
- what continues while offline
- maximum offline duration
- which resources are generated
- whether workers continue their assigned jobs
- how machines progress
- whether market prices advance
- how offline rewards are presented when the player returns


17. ADS / OPTIONAL REWARDS
--------------------------

The current monetization concept includes optional rewarded ads.

Planned rewards include one of:
- 2× game speed for 5 minutes
- 2 Dynamite

Ads should be optional and provide convenience rather than being required for normal progression.

The final monetization strategy still needs to be decided, including whether the game is free, ad-supported, paid, or uses additional purchases.


18. UI / UX
-----------

Primary UI areas:
- main simulation view
- worker assignment panel
- resource/money display
- machine/upgrades interface
- market/selling interface
- prestige interface
- settings
- statistics

The worker panel clearly shows Mining and Logistics worker counts and allows moving individual workers between roles.

UI presents game state and sends player commands. Gameplay rules remain outside the UI.


19. MOBILE LAYOUT
-----------------

Target: Android portrait.

The game should adapt to different screen sizes without changing the core game area unnecessarily.

Taller screens:
- extend the blue-sky/background area
- move UI as necessary
- preserve the main simulation presentation

Wider screens:
- allow UI to become wider without becoming unnecessarily taller
- show more horizontal world area where possible
- preserve the intended vertical game composition

Exact responsive layout rules will be finalized during implementation on multiple Android aspect ratios.


20. ART DIRECTION
-----------------

Visual direction:
- pixel-art inspired
- colorful gemstones
- clear silhouettes
- readable machines and workers
- satisfying explosions and physical motion
- visually distinct gem colors
- attractive rounded mine terrain/surface
- persistent Sand Tower visible in the background

The game should prioritize readability and visual satisfaction over excessive detail.

Gem rendering implementation is undecided: one reusable sprite tinted per gem color or separate sprites.


21. AUDIO
---------

Audio should reinforce physical and economic feedback.

Important sound categories:
- explosions
- rocks falling/impacting
- machine processing
- gems moving/sorting
- selling/reward feedback
- upgrades
- prestige
- UI interaction

Music, ambient audio and the final sound style remain to be defined.


22. TUTORIAL / ONBOARDING
-------------------------

The player should quickly understand:
1. Workers mine.
2. Explosions create Stones.
3. Machines process Stones.
4. Logistics workers move resources.
5. Gems go into color-specific storage.
6. Gems are sold for Money.
7. Money buys progression.

The exact tutorial presentation is not yet defined.


23. STATISTICS
--------------

Useful statistics may include:
- total terrain destroyed
- Stones produced
- Gems produced by color
- Sand produced
- Gems sold
- Money earned
- production rate
- worker count by role
- machine throughput
- play time
- prestige count

Statistics are primarily informational unless explicitly turned into progression mechanics.


24. SAVE / LOAD
--------------

The game requires persistent local saves.

Persistent state should include the gameplay state necessary to continue progression, including:
- money
- workers
- worker assignments
- upgrades
- progression/unlocks
- prestige state
- stored resources
- Sand Tower progress
- relevant world state

Save frequency, backup/corruption handling and multiple save slots remain to be decided.


25. ACCESSIBILITY
-----------------

Minimum goals:
- readable UI at common Android sizes
- clear touch targets
- important information should not depend only on color
- volume controls
- sensible effects/screen-shake options if those effects are added

Additional accessibility features can be added based on actual gameplay needs.


26. TECHNICAL BOUNDARY
----------------------

This GDD intentionally does NOT define detailed code architecture.

The authoritative technical documents define:
- Core vs Simulation
- World / Mine / WorldGrid ownership
- logical resources vs physical resource bodies
- JobSystem vs LogisticsSystem
- system responsibilities
- communication rules
- Godot scene/C# boundaries
- save architecture
- manager policy

See TechnicalArchitecture.txt for those decisions.

The GDD should describe desired gameplay behavior; it should not duplicate implementation details.


27. CURRENT DESIGN PRINCIPLES
-----------------------------

1. The simulation should be satisfying to watch even when the player does nothing.
2. Physical resources should make mining and processing feel tangible.
3. Player decisions should influence production and progression.
4. The game should remain understandable despite becoming more complex.
5. Idle progression should complement, not replace, active interaction.
6. Visual progression should be persistent and obvious.
7. Performance on Android is a design constraint, not an afterthought.
8. New systems should only be added when they create meaningful gameplay.
9. Unconfirmed ideas should remain explicitly marked as undecided rather than becoming accidental requirements.


28. OPEN DESIGN QUESTIONS
--------------------------

These are the remaining questions that should be answered before the corresponding systems are finalized.

HIGH PRIORITY

1. What is the exact long-term mine structure?
   - finite mine with reset/new mine?
   - expanding mine?
   - multiple mine layers/areas?

2. What exactly does a Stone contain?
   - one gem?
   - chance of one of six gems?
   - multiple gems?
   - rarity/quality?

3. What is the exact processing chain?
   - Stone → Grinder → Gem + Sand is the current baseline.

4. What are the meaningful player decisions beyond worker assignment, dynamite and upgrades?

5. What is the final market model and why is saving/selling strategically interesting?

6. What exactly resets at Prestige, and what permanent progression is retained?

7. What is the long-term goal/endgame?

8. What is the final offline progression model?

9. What is the final monetization model?

MEDIUM PRIORITY

10. Are warehouse capacities limited and upgradeable?
11. Can workers have upgrades/traits, or only increased count?
12. Does the Sand Tower have gameplay effects or remain visual progression?
13. Which additional machines, if any, are required for the final game?
14. What randomness/discovery systems exist besides mining and market fluctuation?
15. What are the first 5–10 minutes of progression and upgrades?
16. What is the intended maximum simulation scale on Android?
17. What are the final audio/music goals?
18. What is the final tutorial/onboarding flow?

LOW PRIORITY / IMPLEMENTATION-ADJACENT

19. One tinted gem sprite vs separate gem sprites.
20. Exact responsive layout rules across Android aspect ratios.
21. Exact save backup/multiple-slot behavior.
22. Final accessibility options.
