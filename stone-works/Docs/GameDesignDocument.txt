## 1. Game Vision

* What is the game called? StoneWorks
* What is the one-sentence description? Idle Mining and Gem Selling Game
* What is the core fantasy? I like shiny gemstones.
* What should the player *feel* while playing? He should feel advancement and richness without doing anything.
* What makes the game interesting? The color sorting is beautiful. The physics and explosions are satisfying.
* What makes it different from other games? 
* Who is the target player? People like me. I don't know.
* What platform(s) is it for? Android mainly. 
* Portrait or landscape? Portrait
* Offline or online? Mainly Offline
* What is the intended session length?
* Is it an idle game, incremental game, simulation, management game, or hybrid? Hybrid of all those.
* What is the overall long-term goal?

# The GDD's "master questions"

If you want a much simpler way to think about the whole document, your GDD should eventually answer these **15 questions**:

1. **What is the game?**
2. **Why is it fun?**
3. **Who is it for?**
4. **What does the player do?**
5. **What is the core gameplay loop?**
6. **What can the player control?**
7. **What systems exist?**
8. **How do those systems communicate?**
9. **What resources exist?**
10. **How does progression work?**
11. **What content exists?**
12. **What does the game look and sound like?**
13. **How does the player interact with it?**
14. **What technical constraints must the game satisfy?**
15. **What exactly needs to be built, and in what order?**

For **StoneWorks specifically**, I would *not* try to write the giant GDD all at once. I'd build it in layers:

**Vision → Core Loop → Resources → Mining → Physics → Machines → Progression → Economy → UI → Architecture → Content → Technical Requirements → Roadmap**

That would give you a GDD that is actually useful for development rather than a huge document full of ideas that aren't yet decided.
